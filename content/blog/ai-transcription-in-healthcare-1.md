**The Robodebt crisis showed what happens when our government allows automated systems to make decisions about people’s lives without any safeguards. Three years after the Royal Commission told the government how to stop it from happening again, they have still not fixed the problem.**

The ABC has just released ‘[Shakedown](https://iview.abc.net.au/show/shakedown)’, a TV dramatisation of the shocking events that constitute Robodebt. 

Between 2015 and 2019 the Australian government used an unlawful automated system to claim debts from hundreds of thousands of people on income support. Ordinary people were forced to prove to Centrelink that they didn’t owe money. Lots paid, and some paid with their lives. The Commonwealth eventually settled a class-action lawsuit with a $1.8billion settlement - the largest in Australian history. 

In July 2023 the Royal Commission into Robodebt found the scheme was unlawful and recommended that any future automated decision-making (ADM) programs are regulated under a legislated framework with an independent body to monitor and audit against it.

More than three years later there is still no comprehensive legal framework governing ADM across the provision of Government services, even as they spread through aged care, NDIS, Services Australia, and more. Each use is regulated, if at all, by its own patchwork rules. The gaps are significant:

* No comprehensive audit: No one has mapped where and how government uses ADM
* Not enough transparency: People affected by automated decisions often don’t know that an ADM was involved, or understand how it works
* Human judgement being displaced: ADM blurs lines of responsibility where there is no clear lines of accountability

The people who are most affected by these gaps are those who depend on government services the most: people receiving income support, older people in care, people with a disability, and people seeking safety through the migration system.

**Join our campaign now as we push for a single ADM standard for the whole of government.** This means legislation to cover:

1. Transparency: To allow for scrutiny by the public and independent experts, the government must publish where ADM is used, how it works, & the rules it follows.
2. Human oversight and the power to override: For high-risk decisions, a person with the authority and ability to override the automated decision must be involved in and responsible for the result.
3. A fast, genuine right to review: Anyone affected by an automated decision must be informed and be able to challenge the decision, with a real prospect of the decision being corrected.

After Robodebt the government promised it would never happen again. Promises without action are just words. This promise needs a law behind it.
