In October 2025, Digital Rights Watch partnered with Essential Media to run a national event series exploring how democracy can survive the rise of AI. This report compiles the views of the audience from around Australia on AI, democracy, and the how the march of technological change is impacting their lives.

**What did we find?**

* **Regulation is the priority**: 89% of all responses on how democracy could survive AI focused on five key areas: regulation (30%), education (16%), collective action (14%), accountability (14%), and individual action (9%)
* **Strong appetite for accountability**: Among participants, there was a clear appetite for responsibility to sit with those who design, deploy, and profit from AI systems. 82% of accountability-focused responses centered on holding tech executives or companies responsible
* **Workplaces as sites of resistance**: Participants viewed AI as having the most potential, but also the greatest risk, in the workplace. 50% of collective action responses focused on worker-based action, particularly through unions
* **Collective action and resistance**: 14% of responses referenced collective action, with notable themes around community-owned AI as a form of collective resistance. An addition 9% focused on individual resistance actions.
